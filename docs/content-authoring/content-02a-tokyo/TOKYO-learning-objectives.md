# Tokyo Daily English — learning objectives

**Revision:** TOKYO-DAILY-r1 · **Status:** DRAFT · **Review:** bilingual educator and assessment review pending  
**Use:** authoring proposal for future review; not imported, published, calibrated or learner-tested.

## Scope and evidence boundary

Twelve original Tokyo-trip practice scenarios span airport recovery, rail, convenience shopping, food, city navigation and social repair. Their route boards, times, prices, menu contents and service conditions are explicitly fictional. The only learning source for an individual Tower candidate is its linked ATLAS-authored lesson passage in TOKYO-scenarios.json; source websites below support terminology or real-world framing and did not author the ATLAS dialogue.

A2–B1 is an editorial workload hypothesis, not a validated CEFR placement. Complexity is estimated per scenario with lexical load, sentence complexity, number of information pieces, inference demand, distractor similarity, interaction demand and support. No mastery, fluency or broad speaking claim follows from a correct recognition answer.

## Progression and evidence labels

The progression is a set of evidence choices, not a mandatory ladder each objective must climb:

- **Recognition:** select a taught meaning, sign or response while the relevant passage/map is available.
- **Recall:** retrieve a taught chunk after the passage is hidden or audio is replayed under a defined policy. This is still supported by a familiar scenario.
- **Interpretation:** combine two or more taught details (for example destination + platform + time) without introducing outside facts.
- **Application:** choose, sequence or compose a response for a new but structurally parallel instance. Controlled token ordering remains controlled production.
- **Transfer:** use the same intent in another setting with venue-specific vocabulary and facts taught there first. Tokyo → London/Singapore is a proposal, not evidence yet.

For social repair, a candidate answer must state the intended function rather than reward a single memorized sentence. A future open response would need a human-reviewed acceptable-meaning rubric; no automated free-text scoring is proposed here.
